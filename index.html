About Me

The Origin Hunter is run by Sarah Maywood, an independent family-history researcher with over 15 years’ experience exploring genealogy and historical records.

I don’t just build family trees; I like to put the “meat on the bones” and uncover the people behind the names. I enjoy following records and clues, piecing together relationships, places and events to build a fuller picture of a family’s past.

Every project is researched by me personally, with care, precision and a healthy dose of curiosity. I take the time to follow the evidence, explain what I find clearly and, where the records allow, uncover the stories that make a family tree feel like more than a collection of names and dates.

The Origin Hunter is deliberately personal. You deal directly with me throughout, and your research is never passed on to someone else.

Certificates & Additional Document Costs

Birth, marriage and death indexes can often be located through subscription research sites, but the full certificates themselves are not normally included with those subscriptions. Where a certificate is needed to confirm important details or progress the research, it may need to be purchased separately from the General Register Office or other relevant archive.

I will always discuss any additional document costs with you before ordering.

How It Works

1. Choose your research package

2. Complete the Client Information Form here If you have any questions about the Client Information Form before submitting it, please feel free to email me.

3. I will review the information you have provided and confirm whether I am able to take on the research.

4. If I am able to take on the research, I will confirm the next steps with you by email and send your invoice for the 50% upfront payment.

5. Work begins once the 50% upfront payment has been received. I aim to finish the agreed project within 28 days of receiving payment and everything needed to begin. If it is likely to take longer than this, I will let you know as soon as possible and keep you updated on the expected timeframe.

6. When the agreed research is ready for review, I will send the invoice for the remaining 50%. Once full payment has been received, I will complete the final handover of your tree.

Your family tree will normally be researched and built within your own Ancestry account.

If you do not already have an Ancestry account, you will need to register for one before the research begins. A free account is enough to create and own your family tree and to view the tree structure, names, relationships, timeline notes, stories, photographs and other information I add. Some historical records, documents and record images may require an Ancestry subscription to view in full.

Once your tree has been created, I will need temporary Editor access for the duration of the research. If you already have an Ancestry tree, you can give me temporary Editor access to that instead. I may make reasonable evidence-based additions, corrections or amendments where appropriate.

For the best and most complete result, I recommend using Ancestry. If you do not wish to use Ancestry, I can provide your family tree as a GEDCOM file instead. A GEDCOM transfers core family-tree information such as names, relationships, dates, places and notes, but does not include the actual photographs, document images or other media attached to the tree, and some source information may transfer differently.

Research Disclaimer

Family-history research relies on surviving historical records, which may be incomplete, inaccurate or difficult to interpret. Names and surnames can appear in different forms, handwriting may be unclear, and indexes or online databases can contain transcription errors.

I make every reasonable effort to verify findings by comparing records and sources wherever possible. However, where the surviving evidence is incomplete, unclear or contradictory, absolute accuracy cannot always be guaranteed. If you believe something in your research may be incorrect, please contact me and I will review the available evidence where reasonably possible.
